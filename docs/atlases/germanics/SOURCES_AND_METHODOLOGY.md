# Germanic-labelled and German-speaking migrations: sources and methodology

Prepared 15 November 2026. This document describes the Germanic atlas edition covering **100 BCE–2026 CE**, its retained evidence, its explicit visualization assumptions and its current readable-opening animation. It is intended to accompany the public atlas and to be readable independently of the application.

The edition follows ancient Germanic-labelled coalitions, Scandinavian expansions, medieval settlement movements, later German-speaking communities, forced displacement and overseas migration. These categories do not describe one continuous biological population or a single nation. Ancient ethnic labels, language, archaeological cultures, ancestry, citizenship and modern self-identification are different kinds of evidence. The catalogue covers the supplied report; it is not an exhaustive census of every people ever called Germanic.

## Evidence and access

The starting evidence was a privately supplied synthesis titled *Comprehensive Historical, Demographic, and Geospatial Analysis of German-Speaking and Germanic-Labelled Migrations*. Its author, compilation date and publication venue were not supplied. It contains inline references to scholars, studies, institutions, censuses and archival counts, but **no formal bibliography, URLs, DOIs or numbered footnotes**. Consequently, a name in that report does not establish which publication, edition or page was consulted.

The retained UTF-8 source is 120,869 bytes. Its SHA-256 is `bc15517b2b642f697d30e3c948dd094532b101a33a0c2479c95e5072885385f6`. The private register and prepared research retain the full original wording and its qualifications. This public document describes the evidence without reproducing that full research input.

The current model's historical source links point to the single source ID **REPORT**. They are not a completed network of independently checked, claim-by-claim citations to primary texts. The bibliography below recovers identifiable references and distinguishes them from incomplete leads. A recovered citation does not retroactively prove every statement attributed to it in the supplied synthesis.

Access labels used below:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Targeted text checked** | The original publisher or institutional text was available and the stated passage, table or section was inspected. This does not mean the complete work and every supplement were reviewed. |
| **Metadata checked** | A publisher, journal or institutional catalogue identifies the work. Its relevant argument, numerical table or cited page has not been checked. |
| **Locator only** | An official archive, database or collection is identified, but the particular document or table supporting the supplied claim is missing. |
| **Unresolved** | The supplied mention lacks enough information to establish the exact work, page or edition. A possible match is labelled as a candidate. |
| **Context only** | Relevant background, a methodological comparison or a later discovery, rather than direct evidence for a plotted migration or population. |

The document separates reported movement, dated presence, origin tradition, political or military footprint, and visualization reconstruction. A dated presence is not automatically an arrival; a conquest is not a resident census; an archaeological label is not automatically a language or ethnic identity. Traditions and disputed reconstructions remain qualified.

## Chronology and geographic reconstruction

### Dates

The research layer preserves the supplied chronology, broad periods, competing dates and textual uncertainty. The model assigns explicit windows to render movement where a source does not supply exact departure and arrival years. These are display reconstructions, not new historical observations. Named intermediate stages can receive holds; unnamed control points shape a corridor without asserting a settlement there.

The longer ancient transits in the current edition represent **cohort progression through a reconstructed relocation period**, not the measured duration of one person's journey. Their moving model years are A1-STAGE 60; A1-VIS 24; A1-OST 9; A2 24; A3 5; A4 15; A5 60; A6 60; A7 22; A8 60. Holds are retained and the final arrivals remain inside the existing source windows. The change makes the same people visibly move; it does not add migrants or change the retained evidence dates.

### Corridors

The builder uses named origins, staging regions and destinations, then reconstructs connected land, coastal, river and maritime approaches. Mode-specific geometry prevents a land migration being represented by an arbitrary straight line across water. Maritime control points describe a navigable display corridor; they are not evidence for exact ship tracks. The global land mask includes the Americas, Atlantic islands and Greenland, which the earlier regional mask did not cover.

Examples of explicit geographic choices include a Ravensburg-area origin for the Sathmar migration, separate Norwegian and Danish/Swedish departure pools, separate European and Volga embarkation pools, and a Mexico–Paraguay relay through the Caribbean, Atlantic, Río de la Plata and Paraguay approaches. These choices are reconstructions linked to the relevant record, not surveyed itineraries.

The preparation audit sampled all 129 land-only journeys and found no sampled water segment. This is a software/geometric check against the retained modern land mask. It does not establish the historical correctness of those routes, the suitability of every segment or the absence of water at every possible sampling resolution.

### Irregular occupied footprints

Homelands and destinations use deterministic, irregular candidate footprints rather than one point, circular clouds or land-clipped discs. The placement module follows retained Natural Earth river and coastline geometry, variable-width resource ribbons and connected branches. Explicit lowland, foothill and water-access guide belts supplement coarse global hydrography. Each prepared origin, destination or reservoir pool samples 384 candidates; later placement spreads people across the relevant region.

Selected coarse wetland and delta cores are excluded. River-derived candidates are offset from central channels, but coarse geometry and other guide belts do not identify every waterbody. The wetland exclusions cover parts of the lower Danube, Pripyat, Vasyugan, lower Ob, Volga delta, Pantanal, Iberá and eastern Chaco. The boundaries and resource weights are display assumptions. They do not amount to a complete wetland inventory, ancient climate reconstruction, digital elevation analysis, slope-cost model or mapped historic settlement boundary.

Modern land and river data are geographic proxies. The atlas does not reconstruct all ancient shorelines, river channels, land reclamation, land use, seasonal water or changing settlement capacity. Terrain shading provides context; it is not a hidden demographic suitability calculation. A dot within a broad footprint does not claim that a specific household occupied that coordinate. See the [placement module](../../../scripts/germanic-landscapes.mjs) and [land-mask implementation](../../../scripts/route-landmask.mjs).

## Population estimates and accounting

### Source values and visualization scenarios

All 37 original source-population fields remain `null`. The supplied report also contains numerical anchors, but those anchors mix migrants, warriors, households, farmsteads, resident stocks, descendants, deaths, census identities, ancestry percentages and competing reconstructions. They are retained with their counting units rather than forced into one uniform source headcount.

The separate [population model](../../../site/atlas/data/research/germanic-migrations.population.json) gives each visual cohort a **low, central and high** scenario, method, basis and relevant inclusion or parent relationships. These are scenario choices, not statistical confidence intervals. Source-free allocations are illustrative. Missing evidence does not become a single surrogate dot, and a population percentage does not become a migrant count without a denominator and a stated conversion.

The model contains 197 estimate records, including 26 non-rendered aggregate or inclusion aliases. Their rows must not be summed to obtain unique migrants: aliases, parent populations and successive legs can describe the same people. Some scenario endpoints reflect different published scopes rather than a single common sampling distribution.

| Transylvanian Saxon farmsteads | 520 farmsteads × 5 people = 2,600 as one anchor. The central 15,000 and high 25,000 visualization choices have a broader reconstructed scope; they are not implied by that multiplication alone. |
| Medieval Prussia | The M3 allocation is included inside the broader M1/Kuhn 200,000 framework, not added on top as another full cohort. |
| Sathmar and Danube Swabians | E2 is included in the E4 umbrella rather than duplicated in both totals. |
| Volga settlers | 26,676 dispatched is distinguished from 23,383 survivors and 3,293 deaths. The central dispatch-based display is not a literal count of survivors. |
| Bessarabia, Baltic and Central Asian anchors | A later resident census, a social-group stock or net stock change is not treated as a founder flow without an additional model assumption. In particular, the 8,846 Bessarabian figure and 41,477 Kazakhstan change retain their separate scopes. |
| Anglo-Saxon genetic studies | Cemetery and present-day ancestry proportions support bounded historical discussion, not a 1:1 count of migrants. The atlas's 50,000–250,000 scenario is a separate visualization reconstruction. |

### Transfers, retention and later generations

The [journey model](../../../site/atlas/data/research/germanic-migrations.journeys.json) and [standing ledger](../../../site/atlas/data/research/germanic-migrations.ledger.json) link departures, intermediate stages, arrivals and onward transfers. A connected movement debits its parent or source pool; an arrival does not create an extra copy beside an unchanged full parent. A group can retain a residual population while an allocated branch continues elsewhere.

The Germanic ledger sets continuous natural growth, losses and additions to zero. Later source generations enter as explicit dated baseline priors. This avoids pretending that medieval founders automatically grow into a twentieth-century census through an undocumented fertility model. It also means the display is **a cumulative migration-history cohort ledger, not a complete living-population census**. It does not simulate every birth, death, assimilation, identity change or intermarriage.

There are 87 dated priors. Their central amounts sum to 26,135,394 across dates and generations. That bookkeeping sum is not a count of unique people ever migrating and is not the resident population at any one date. Exact dot counts can include a partially filled final dot.

Source-reservoir sensitivity has separate declared scales: the low-flow display retains 1× the central prior while departures use their low estimates; high source funding uses 5× the prior. These factors are arbitrary funding/display assumptions, not observed census bounds or confidence intervals. They must not be confused with the low/central/high estimates for individual journey flows.

### Wartime and return accounting

- **W1:** the supplied 1,009,113 total includes the 662,448 subset. The Ukrainian 350,000 framework is allocated as 300,000 linked repatriations and 50,000 postwar-expulsion allocation; alternative 280,000–310,000 reconstructions are correlated alternatives, not extra populations.
- **W2:** 799,459 / 950,000 / 1,209,430 are low/central/high frameworks with differing source scopes. The 315,000 Trudarmiya count is a subset, and release/return flows debit earlier arrivals. The prewar Siberia–Kazakhstan stock of 231,301 is not silently relabelled as wartime deportees.
- **W3:** the 271,672 component-based lower count, 295,000 central scenario and 600,000 upper framework represent different definitions of foreign civilian internment. The 201,464 return allocation draws from earlier arrivals. Administrative totals, mortality and individual outcomes are not interchangeable.
- **W4:** the scoped 1950 displacement flow uses 11.6 million / 12.3 million / 12.75 million scenarios. Later 14.6 million and 16.22 million stocks including offspring are not treated as additional migrant flows. Expulsion losses and unresolved fates require separate definitions and are not totalled as though they were identical categories.
- **W5:** the BVA table gives **4,481,882 admissions for 1950–2005**, partitioned into former USSR 2,334,334; Poland 1,444,847; Romania 430,101; former CSFR 105,095; former Yugoslavia 90,378; Hungary 21,411; other countries 55,716. These components sum to the total. [BVA one-page statistical table](https://www.mi.niedersachsen.de/download/83330/Aussiedlerstatistik_von_1950_-_2005.pdf).

For the former-USSR W5 component, the model first links available older W2/R7 parent balances using a fixed 80% of the minimum available parent balance across scenarios, then supplies an explicit later-generation/unlinked residual. **The 80% is a bookkeeping choice, not an observed survival, return or descent rate.** Other origin-country baselines are also explicit. This prevents the model from identifying every later admission with a specific surviving wartime deportee.

The overseas primary flows use 6 million / 6.75 million / 7.5 million scenarios allocated once across destinations. Mennonite relays debit the relevant Canadian, Mexican, Manitoban or Brazilian parents; the same population is not copied onto each onward route. Modern ancestry responses in the Americas, Africa or Australia are context, not historical immigration totals.

## Dots, camera and playback

### What a dot means

The default unit is **1,000 people per dot**: 50,000 modelled people means 50 dots. Dot size is independent of that unit. The current default scale is 2.4, with a minimum radius of 2.7 screen pixels and a 1-pixel black (`#000000`) outline. A final partially filled dot represents a remainder; it is not rounded into another full thousand people.

The default colour mode distinguishes the 37 stable group keys. Dots are visible at their origin, progress along hidden geographic corridors, and persist at arrival until an explicit linked transfer moves them. Paths are hidden by default. The small question mark in the compact caption indicates reconstruction; uncertain population estimates are not faded away.

Clicking a dot opens its journey and source details while retaining the current visible population. Explicit group controls perform filtering. Changing the dot-size control changes visibility, not the number of people represented. A one-time display revision upgrades the old saved size below 2.4 while preserving larger manual values and subsequent manual choices.

### Steady automatic framing

The current camera plan opens on occupied ground and expands as people move and settle. Future route endpoints do not force the opening out to the final global extent. The plan retains previously occupied ground and eases outward rather than recentering on a newly calculated bounding box every frame or zooming inward as one edge empties.

Planning uses samples at approximately four per playback second plus exact activation and arrival boundaries. Filters, viewport dimensions, reverse scrubbing and restart are included in deterministic plan selection. Manual pan or zoom suspends automatic framing; Home resumes it at the current date. The [camera implementation](../../../src/map/population-camera.ts) is a display method, not an inference about historical territorial extent.

### Current four-interval clock

The current Germanic edition uses an explicit piecewise clock so the early dots themselves remain visible in motion. The matching default playback and finished export use this schedule:

| Historical interval | Rate | Playback duration | Cumulative time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 BCE–100 CE | 200 model years/second | 1.00 s | 1.00 s |
| 100–400 CE | 15 model years/second | 20.00 s | 21.00 s |
| 400–600 CE | 5 model years/second | 40.00 s | 61.00 s |
| 600–2026 CE | 25 model years/second | 57.04 s | 118.04 s |

These are the application's numeric year intervals; the playback calculation is not a new calendrical dating claim. Explicit intervals bypass legacy adaptive slowdowns and idle/travel modifiers. There is no static title or ending padding. The 24-fps endpoint produces a measured **118.083333-second** MP4. Earlier 97-second, two-phase exports are superseded.

Calendar speed alone does not establish visible travel. The completed export checks the same stable migration-and-slot identity across adjacent frames, requires intrinsic route progress on every transition, confirms painted group-colour pixels, excludes stationary holds and camera-only motion, and requires movement beyond the dot diameter. Thirty uninterrupted sequences pass. The first tracked Gothic dot has 28 moving transitions, or 1.166667 seconds, travelling 372 rendered pixels; a longer Migration Period sequence lasts 11.958333 seconds. These are rendering measurements, not ancient travel-speed estimates.

## Limitations and corrections

The supplied synthesis is the atlas's evidence input, not a peer-reviewed conclusion adopted wholesale. Bibliographic recovery has identified concrete scope problems that must remain visible:

- Mitochondrial, Y-chromosome and genome-wide evidence answer different questions. A cemetery sample is not the entire population bearing an archaeological or ethnic label.
- The Gretzinger study's early-medieval eastern English estimate and its present-day English ancestry range refer to different samples and dates. They must not be combined into one migrant headcount.
- Kinship inside a cemetery, shared ancestry between sites and direct biological kinship linking named sites are different claims. The supplied Lombard discussion is stronger than the targeted checked passages establish.
- Space-voyage or generic founder-viability simulations do not count historical Norse settlers. The supplied Moore attribution remains incomplete; a related later citation cannot silently become the exact missing source.
- Source stocks, migration flows, later generations, deaths and missing persons cannot be added indiscriminately. Administrative classifications and political conditions also affect who appears in a census or displacement register.
- Indigenous and already resident populations, non-Germanic participants, local intermarriage and changing identities are not fully modelled. The surviving dots are migration-history accounting, not exclusive occupation, ethnic replacement or an assertion of unchanged identity through 2026.
- Regional labels, reconstructed release schedules, destination shares, resource belts and travel geometry remain assumptions. They are disclosed separately from the source's original claims.

An improved historical edition would attach exact passage/table identifiers to each source claim, replace unresolved authority mentions with complete references, reconcile competing definitions and commission a route-by-route review. This edition already provides explicit estimates and connected geography so the animation remains inspectable while those historical questions stay open.

The public bibliography and visualization explanation may be shared with the public atlas. The full supplied source, original register and embedded original research are retained in the private research version. Publication must preserve that boundary and must not expose private input merely because its local preparation path contains `public/`.
