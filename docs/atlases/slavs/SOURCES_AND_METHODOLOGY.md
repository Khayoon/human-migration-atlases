# Slavic dispersals: sources and methodology

This atlas follows reconstructed Slavic-associated population movements from **200 BCE to 1000 CE**, with dispersal beginning in the fifth century. It combines an archaeological and historical source register with a separate geographic reconstruction and an illustrative population model. It is an animated explanation of one reconstruction, not a census or a map of fixed medieval ethnic boundaries. Research preparation, implementation and documentation were AI-assisted.

**Edition described:** the western Balkan routes / quick opening edition, prepared on 15 November 2026. It contains **23 journeys, 24 standing population pools and 30 event captions**. Its latest checked video is `Slavic-Dispersals-western-balkan-routes-quick-opening-2026-11-15.mp4`: **64.791667 seconds**, with actual movement beginning at **1.25 seconds**. Earlier exports and the old all-Penkovka southern routing are superseded.

The bibliography below distinguishes published research from competing hypotheses, ancient testimony transmitted through scholarship, modern geographic data and incomplete leads. A citation establishes only the claim described beside it. It does not independently verify every dot, route, date or population estimate.

## What the map represents

The retained register contains **21 source records**: three archaeological context records P1–P3; fourteen regional records W1–W4, E1–E4 and S1–S6; and four later modules L1–L4. Every source headcount remains unknown (`null`). The 23 journeys subdivide or stage these records; they are not 23 independently counted peoples. The 30 estimate rows include seven non-rendering aliases, not thirty separate cohorts.

P1 is the Zarubintsy horizon. P2 is the Gothic-associated Wielbark horizon, retained as context rather than counted as a Slavic migrant population. P3 covers Kyiv, Zaozer’e and Cherepyn–Teremtsy horizons within a broad ancestral proxy. These labels describe archaeological categories and their interpretation; pottery, ancestry, external names, language and political affiliation are different kinds of evidence.

The opening footprint is a broad Polesia–Dnieper/Desna reconstruction. The Pripyat/Pripet/Prypiat name refers to the wetland region, not a precise, demonstrated point of origin. A later West/East/South Slavic language classification is not automatically the identity of every sixth-century community. Modern names such as Ukraine, Poland or Russia locate regions for readers; modern borders do not define the reconstructed communities.

L1–L4 retain later eastward expansion, Balkan/Habsburg frontier movements, imperial/overseas dispersals and twentieth-century forced transfers. They are outside this first-millennium animation. Their presence in the register does not place later populations on the medieval map.

The public explanation records source identifiers and bibliographic information. The full supplied report and private working history belong in the private source/archive copy; they are not reproduced here.

## Evidence and claim types

| Evidence type | What it can support here | What the animation must not infer from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contemporary or near-contemporary narrative | Named groups, encounters, raids or presence in a broad area | An exact migration itinerary, resident headcount or continuous ethnic polygon |
| Dated inscription or annal entry | A name, title, ruler or group attested by that date | That all residents arrived in that year, or shared one ancestry |
| Archaeological settlement or cemetery horizon | Dated material practices and regional occupation; sometimes settlement change | That a material culture is a single language, nation or genetically uniform population |
| Ancient DNA | Ancestry affinities and changes among sampled individuals, with sampling/model limitations | Exact departure places, a complete census, or a direct ancestry-percentage-to-migrant-count conversion |
| Dietary/isotopic/lipid evidence | Changes in practices and provisioning in sampled contexts | An independent headcount or a route traversed by every family |
| Retrospective origin tradition | A later account of origins or political identity | Contemporary documentation of the narrated migration |
| Geographic reconstruction | A plausible connection between regional evidence through land/river corridors | Proof that a source documents those intermediate coordinates |

The register preserves textual chronology, qualifications and inherited/unreviewed status. S1 and S3 include reported frontier presence; the visible connecting paths remain reconstructions. W3's 846 attestation and W4's 806 Sorabi attestation are not arrival dates. Branimir's late ninth-century inscriptions are attestations, not a census. Constantine VII's Croatian/Serbian origin narratives are retrospective. Avar political association does not by itself count Slavic residents or identify their ancestry.

The preparation does not set a reviewed-source flag merely to admit a reconstruction. All 23 geographic journeys remain inherited/unreviewed. Details identify reconstructed paths and dates. Limited checks of selected primary publications are separated from full historical verification.

## Population model and accounting

### Units and scenario assumptions

The supplied synthesis gives normalized flow units (**NFU**), not historical headcounts. The display independently chooses **1 NFU = 1,000 people**. The following are visualization scenarios, not confidence intervals or measured medieval population estimates.

| Quantity | Low | Central | High |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Standing ancestral proxy cohort | 800,000 | 1,400,000 | 2,000,000 |
| Unique cumulative departure from the core | 600,000 | 1,000,000 | 1,600,000 |
| Remaining core at 1000 CE | 200,000 | 400,000 | 400,000 |

The source gives an 800–2,000 NFU standing-core band. Choosing its midpoint of 1,400 NFU, and converting NFU to people, are display decisions. The source's ancestry percentages and unknown local-baseline bands remain separate evidence/model fields. The current animation does **not** solve the report's ancestry-to-flow equation, fit fertility or local retention, or calibrate an empirical census from genetic percentages.

The source prose discusses growth multipliers and competing demographic interpretations. The implemented ledger instead fixes **natural growth at zero**, applies no modeled mortality losses or descendant additions, and tracks a conserved migrant-origin cohort. It does not add a speculative pre-existing local population. This makes transfer accounting inspectable; it does not model the full biological demography of a millennium. A dot represents a population accounting unit, not one literally immortal family.

### Funded transfers

Every departure debits a standing source and credits an arrival pool after transit. Source residents remain at origin until they depart; arrived residents persist unless a funded onward movement removes them. The engine uses shared transfer events and stable dot slots to prevent the same numbered resident being displayed simultaneously as an origin resident, traveller and destination resident. Fractional final dots preserve quantities when the chosen people-per-dot unit does not divide the total.

The current inclusive primary budgets are B1 **315/525/835 NFU**, B2 **205/345/555**, and B3 **80/130/210**. Together they equal the unique root outflow. B4 transfers **50/85/140** from B1 through northern Carpathian, Moravian and western Pannonian staging; it supplies S2-A and S6 and ends empty. This does not remove or duplicate W3's retained residents.

S4's **85/140/225** includes S5's **25/45/75**, leaving **60/95/150** in S4. S5 may depart available residents while S4 is still receiving later arrivals; it does not wait for the entire parent wave to finish. E3's **60/100/160** includes E3-D's **8/15/25**, leaving **52/85/135**. B3 retains E2's **30/50/80** while sending E4's **50/80/130** onward.

For every modeled date and scenario, the central accounting identity is:

```text
standing core + standing staged/settled cohort + travelling cohort = initial cohort
```

Summing all journey totals would count the same people repeatedly as they pass through staging pools. Neither route counts nor catalogue row counts are population totals.

## Routes and settlement footprints

### Connecting regional evidence

Guide coordinates join named basins, lowlands, foothills and passes. The preparation connects them with a retained present-day land mask. It is not an excavation-derived itinerary or an optimization against measured medieval travel costs. The corridor builder itself does not infer a historically correct river crossing or mountain pass; those choices enter through reconstructed guide legs.

Departure dates are spread over broad source or reconstructed periods. The generic display transit duration is `min(90, max(8, corridor_km / 125))` years unless a journey explicitly overrides it. This is an animation convention for regional dispersal, not a claim about walking speed. Last arrivals can occur after a listed release interval: E3-D releases during 775–950 and arrives approximately 783–958; S2-B releases during 675–698 and arrives approximately 685–708. These examples must not be retold as documentary arrival dates.

The southwestern model now includes B1/B4 northern Carpathian–Moravian–western Pannonian staging, the first Alpine pulse and a late Dalmatian approach. Southeastern/Lower Danubian routes and an eastern approach through the broader Avar-era Pannonian basin remain. The source allows B1 **and** B2 contributions. Assigning these existing allocations to corridors is a reconstruction, not a measured western/eastern ancestry percentage. There is no basis here for treating every Balkan settler as exclusively Penkovka-derived, or for asserting a quantified West Slavic majority across the entire Balkans.

The eastern display includes Upper Oka terraces, a Smolensk–Dnieper–Western Dvina connection, retained Ilmen/Volkhov occupation, the Seym/Kursk region and a separately dated Upper Don continuation. These placements do not fill the entire steppe with an assumed Slavic population. The Upper Don continuation uses the later archaeological horizon and does not create new people.

### How dots occupy land

Each of the 24 pools has **4,096 deterministic candidate positions**. These candidates are not 4,096 villages or an additional population. They are reproducible display positions from which the engine takes enough slots for the chosen count.

The resource-shaped sampler uses retained Natural Earth river curves, hand-authored tributary/terrace guides, irregular habitat envelopes and varying corridor widths. Land tests reject water candidates. Explicit lake/exclusion polygons remove unsuitable areas, including a Lake Ilmen water polygon. Wetland polygons can downweight suitability rather than categorically forbidding occupation. A deterministic spatial texture prevents uniform ribbon density; it is synthetic, not measured soil quality.

The Pripyat display distributes candidates among southern raised margins, northern raised margins, Dnieper/Desna terraces and a small levee/island component. Their display shares are 42%, 18%, 35% and 5%. These are habitat-placement priors, not ancestry fractions or surveyed settlement frequencies. The method avoids both uniform circles and a claim that wetland interiors were entirely empty.

River terraces, cultivable lowlands, lower foothills, pasture/water access and landward coastal belts are regional suitability assumptions. No quantitative historical climate raster, soil-yield model, reconstructed carrying-capacity surface, measured water availability or DEM-derived least-cost slope model is implemented. Modern terrain hillshade and land cover in the basemap provide visual context; they do not feed a numerical migration or settlement optimizer.

The route report checks modern land sampling every 2 km with 1 km coastal slack. Its zero-water result for these 23 paths is a software/geometric check, not proof of traversability or historical route use. Present coastlines, river courses, impoundments and wetlands are not reconstructed first-millennium hydrology.

## Animation and export clock

**One dot defaults to 1,000 display people.** Dot magnification is a separate setting: the Slavic default is **2.6**, with a black outline of **1.1 CSS pixels** and a minimum radius of **3.2 CSS pixels**. Larger dots improve visibility without increasing the population. Changing people per dot changes the number of dots while preserving their weighted population total; changing magnification changes only their appearance.

The central cohort is therefore 1,400 full 1,000-person dot equivalents across origin, transit and settled stocks. Destinations accumulate gradually and remain occupied. The current model has no confidence-based fading of estimated people. A small question mark provides access to uncertainty/context; exact quantities and source qualifications stay in the model and details.

The default legend distinguishes destination regions; group colours are another organizational view. Colours do not prove genetic clades, national boundaries or fixed early West/East/South Slavic identities. Routes guide movement but stay hidden by default. The animation uses people dots rather than one marker per migration, arrows, political territory fills or population rings.

The opening is close to Pripyat, approximately `[24, 47.5, 35, 55]` west/south/east/north bounds. It widens smoothly during 425–505 CE to `[10.5, 36.2, 40.5, 59.8]`. The transition begins from the previous geographic frame to prevent a zoom-in snap. Manual pan/zoom suspends automatic framing; the existing resume/reset-view control returns to automatic framing at the current date. Current filters and reverse scrubbing are included in camera checks.

The small title/date box is at top right; a compact two-column legend occupies the bottom right over Anatolia. The finished video checks every positive population dot against both overlay boxes and the viewport. This is a visibility test, not evidence of historical accuracy.

| Clock phase | Historical interval | Rate | Motion-clock duration |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Brief archaeological context | 200 BCE–425 CE | 500 years/second | 1.25 seconds |
| Dispersal | 425–1000 CE | 10 years/second | 57.5 seconds |
| Ending caption completion | Calendar remains at 1000 CE | No added historical movement | 6 seconds in the checked export |

Historical motion lasts **58.75 seconds**. A minimum two-second ending period extends only as needed to finish queued reading holds. The checked inclusive 24 fps export has **1,555 frames / 64.791667 seconds**; the last frame timestamp is 64.75 seconds. This edition's explicitly requested 500/10 schedule overrides the repository's generic 20–50-year/second second-phase guideline. There is no hidden adaptive slowdown. Matching default app playback uses the prepared phases; independent saved manual preferences remain separate.

Pripyat is passive introductory text for six display seconds. The 23 movement/staging captions and seven context/attestation captions are passive labels with 17 px text, 14 px dates and up to 270 px width. Four can appear on a desktop/export, two on a narrow screen. Each reading hold lasts eight display seconds, starting when the caption is placed; queued captions can remain after a short source window ends. Their printed historical dates do not change. Held captions keep a screen rectangle and relocate only if needed to avoid a growing overlay. Reverse scrubbing, restart and filter changes reset stale queues. Captions do not capture clicks, create extra populations or turn attestations into arrivals.

## Disagreements and limitations

1. **Homeland:** a broad ancestry/source proxy does not establish a bounded Urheimat. The supplied synthesis sometimes presents the debate as resolved more completely than the studies support. Contemporary fifth/sixth-century reference genomes remain scarce, partly because cremation limits available sampling.
2. **Sampling and proxies:** sampled cemeteries do not represent every settlement or social group. Genetic proxies from other regions or periods are analytical comparators, not a literal origin coordinate for every migrant. The Olalde study's Eastern-European-related proxy includes early medieval individuals from western Hungary, Czechia, eastern Austria and western Slovakia.
3. **Poland and Moravia:** the Stolarek interpretation is retained as a competing analysis; it is not summed with newer ancestry estimates. The Moravian transect supports change but does not provide this atlas's exact immigrant count or a universally precise turnover percentage.
4. **Alpine chronology:** an early Mura/Drava pattern after about 500 and later Sava pattern around the late seventh century are not identical to Žužek's proposed 595–599/600 northern-route chronology. The current 525–590 and 675–698 release intervals are declared display reconstructions, not a merger of those hypotheses into one documented date.
5. **Balkan waves and origin traditions:** the Frontiers three-wave scheme is a hypothesis/reanalysis. Its late-wave figure/text chronology is not wholly consistent; the current 750–885 S6 release interval is one explicit choice. Retrospective Croatian/Serbian traditions and Branimir's titles cannot by themselves establish the ancestry and itinerary of an entire population.
6. **Upper Don and northern Russia:** mixed early material and later Borshevo evidence must remain distinct. Regional culture, Slavic-tradition craftwork and ancestry affinity do not prove exclusive ethnic occupation, a continuous block of settlements or the atlas's component weights.
7. **Counts and people:** the NFU conversion, midpoint, internal shares, zero-growth ledger and absent local baseline are display choices. They are not calibrated demographic inference. Low/central/high bounds are scenarios, not statistical error bars.
8. **Geography and timing:** candidate dots are not surveyed villages. Modern land clipping and habitat envelopes cannot recover exact medieval rivers, resources, roads, coastlines or years of arrival. The route shapes, transit durations and component shares need independent specialist review.
9. **Alternatives:** the app preserves competing source interpretations in its evidence, but this Slavic edition has no implemented toggle between whole alternative homeland/route or language-family scenarios. Low/central/high estimate modes alter counts within the same reconstruction. A source recommendation is not an implemented feature.
