# Arab migrations and settlement, 853 BCE–1500 CE: sources and methodology

I made an animated reconstruction of Arab migrations and settlement using a historical research register and a separate demographic/geographic model. Research preparation and implementation were AI-assisted.

**The historical evidence and the animation's numerical assumptions are different layers.** This is one reconstruction, not an ancient census, an ethnic map of every inhabitant, or proof of exact itineraries.

## Evidence and scope

The register has 310 catalogue entries: 131 main entries, 151 inherited Egyptian entries and 28 inherited Iraqi entries. The population model treats 287 as within its before-1500 scope. These are not independent tribes: records include branches, military units, ruling households and overlapping observations.

The pre-200 CE story was added separately. The 853 BCE opening follows the Assyrian mention of Gindibu's Arabs at Qarqar. Their mapped distribution across northwestern Arabia, Jordan, the Negev and a smaller Sinai component is reconstructed; Qarqar does not establish that entire homeland or its population.

Arabic names, original wording, broad dates, conflicting estimates and source limitations are retained. Reported movement, dated presence, tradition and reconstruction remain distinguishable. A presence attestation is not automatically an arrival date. Some presence-only records receive inferred inflows for continuous animation; these are not source-documented migrations.

## Dots and population estimates

**1 dot nominally represents 1,000 people:** a 50,000-person wave has 50 dots. A smaller residual can occupy a partial unit. Origin, travelling and settled dots represent the same cohort. Departure debits the source; arrival credits the destination. Settlements persist.

Usable reported counts take priority. The central conversion is **5 people per household-equivalent**, or **5 associated people per warrior** when constructing a settlement cohort from a military anchor. The latter does not mean a source counted four accompanying civilians per soldier. Resident stocks, military rosters and migrant flows are kept distinct.

Missing counts use declared reference-class priors:

| Formation | Low | Central | High |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ruling household/entourage | 20 | 100 | 400 |
| Merchant/maritime community | 100 | 500 | 2,000 |
| Local branch/lineage | 400 | 2,000 | 8,000 |
| Regional tribal contingent | 1,000 | 5,000 | 20,000 |
| Major relocation/garrison pool | 4,000 | 20,000 | 80,000 |
| Broad confederation | 10,000 | 50,000 | 200,000 |

These are order-of-magnitude assumptions, not measured medieval tribe sizes. Low/central/high values are sensitivity scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. The methodological references below discuss indirect estimation; they do not establish these particular numbers.

Umbrella records, children and repeated observations share conserved pools. A split divides the parent; it never copies the full total to every destination. Summing all catalogue rows would double-count people.

## Standing population and growth

The active scenario starts with **500,000 people in 853 BCE**, from 100,000 assumed household-equivalents ×5. This is a model prior, not an estimate derived from Gindibu's camels. The same stock is allocated to regional reservoirs; migration transfers people rather than creating them.

Net growth follows a capacity-limited logistic rule:

    N_next = K / [1 + (K/N - 1) × exp(-r × elapsed_years)]

K is an assumed regional capacity. The central intrinsic rate is **0.2515% annually before capacity limitation**. Growth is held constant at/above capacity; closed roster/elite cohorts do not automatically generate descendants.

Capacities are model choices. The growth rate was selected to fund the specified departures without negative source populations, not calibrated against independent historical censuses or fertility series. Low/high scenarios are fitted separately. The final headcount is therefore a scenario output, not an established total for all Arabs in 1500.

## Hilal and Sulaym

The combined **1,000,000 cumulative migrants**, with a maximum **50,000 per wave**, is a chosen visualization scenario, not a consensus historical count. Competing published estimates remain in the research. The 500,000/1,500,000 sensitivity bounds are a chosen envelope around that scenario.

The aggregate is allocated once across related groups and stages from Arabia through Egypt into Libya, Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco. Branches and Egyptian observations do not each receive another million.

## Geography, dates and presentation

Routes combine stated origins/stages/destinations with named corridor controls, present-day land polygons and river geometry. Land-mask searches avoid open-water shortcuts; maritime legs are separate. This is a geographic reconstruction, **not a calibrated climate-and-slope least-cost model**.

Settlement placement favours oases, wadis, coasts, grazing approaches and dry river plains. These weights are hand-built; modern environmental references do not establish ancient ethnic densities. No population is calculated by multiplying a broad destination polygon by density.

For example, the Syrian/Mesopotamian candidate footprint uses illustrative weights of 15% oasis/pastoral approaches, 25% Euphrates terraces, 20% Tigris terraces, 20% Jazira and 20% central/lower alluvium. Placement changes conserve the stock. Those weights are not census proportions.

Broad periods become reconstructed movement windows; precise animation years and route vertices are not documented departure dates or stops. Modern shorelines/channels are proxies. The restricted pre-630 Mesopotamian distribution, gradual 630–670 opening, southwest-Yemen opening at 600 and Gulf-head shoreline treatment are scenario conventions, not proof of earlier absence. Socotra's inclusion does not imply that all islanders were Arabs or Arabic-speaking.

Brown denotes the pre-Islamic cohort category; post-Islamic colours denote destinations. The video runs at **200 years/second before 630 CE, then 20 years/second to 1500**, lasting **50.958 seconds**. This is presentation timing, not travel speed.
